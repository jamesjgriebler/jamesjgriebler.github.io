About

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University of Tennessee Knoxville under Prof. Ria D. Corder. I study how soft materials – pastes, gels, suspensions, printing inks – store and release deformation as they flow. Pushed gently, these materials spring back; pushed harder, they flow and stay put. Most measurements report one number with both behaviors mixed together. My work separates them, using rheology alongside x-ray and neutron scattering so that a mechanical measurement and a structural one describe the same sample under the same history.

I completed my Ph.D. at institution, where I developed methods that distinguish the deformation a material gives back from the deformation it keeps, and showed that yield-stress fluids flow below the stress at which they are said to yield. Since then I have worked on the structure of protein gels, the rheology of printable pastes, and the link between a material’s mechanical memory and its microstructure. More on where that goes next.
